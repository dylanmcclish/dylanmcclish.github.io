in optimal learning, after modeling the objective and dynamics of a problem, one is confronted with the inevitable need to actually solve said problem for an optimal solution. in these situations, we are often given a problem that involves multiple coupled equations. namely, it is a system of equations that we must optimize.

to solve this, it is often extremely convenient to use an ansatz. an ansatz is simply an educated guess of the solution. we can use an ansatz to solve for the form of one of the variables, then go through a substitution procedure to derive the form of the other variables.

now, i move onto the brain. something that i learned recently was a very basic overview of the method of sensory processing of the human brain. specifically, how we update our view of the world given our surroundings. given input, our brain computes the most likely outcome of the world and then this is what we “perceive”. however, this computation is astronomically taxing for our brain. in response, we have developed the capacity to iteratively compute predictions of what we should be perceiving, and then use the difference between what we are perceiving and what we have in order to update what we perceive. evidently, i don’t understand this too well.

however, conceptually this intrigues me bc/ these are adjacent concepts. the saving of computation cost through the educated guess of a solution prior to solving. not only is this done in optimal learning, but is reflected in our own biology.
